# Western Sahara

## Summary

The English Wikipedia article on Western Sahara, a United Nations–designated
non-self-governing territory in north-western Africa of roughly 272,000 km². The
article describes it as the last African territory yet to achieve independence and
the most sparsely populated in Africa, largely desert flatland, with a population
around 565,000.

**Status and dispute.** The territory's sovereignty is unresolved and the article
presents the competing positions rather than settling them. Spain colonized it as
the Spanish Sahara from 1884 until 1975–76, then attempted to transfer administration
to Morocco and Mauritania — despite an International Court of Justice advisory opinion
finding that neither state had sovereignty over it at the time of Spanish colonization,
and that its population therefore held a right of self-determination. Morocco's Green
March followed in November 1975. War erupted, the Polisario Front proclaimed the
Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR), Mauritania withdrew its claims in 1979, and
Morocco secured *de facto* control of most of the territory. Spain remains the *de
jure* administering power on the UN's list.

Today roughly 70% is under Moroccan control and 30% under the Polisario Front,
separated by the Berm — a 2,700 km mine-lined wall. A UN-sponsored ceasefire took hold
in 1991, but the referendum that MINURSO was created to monitor has never been held,
stalled through the Settlement Plan and the Baker Plan over the unresolved question of
who is eligible to vote. The UN recognizes the Polisario Front as the legitimate
representative of the Sahrawi people; the SADR holds partial recognition and African
Union membership and maintains a government-in-exile at Tindouf, Algeria. Algeria is
Polisario's principal backer; Morocco is supported by France and the United States,
and the article notes growing support for Morocco's autonomy proposal during the 2020s.

**Economy.** Fishing employs about two-thirds of the workforce. The Bou Craa phosphate
mine — served by the world's longest conveyor belt at 98 km — produces roughly 10% of
Morocco's phosphate output while representing under 2% of its proven reserves.
Offshore oil and gas remain speculative. The article devotes substantial space to the
legal ambiguity this creates: a 2002 UN Office of Legal Affairs opinion by Hans Corell
on resource exploitation in a non-self-governing territory, the withdrawal of oil
companies under shareholder pressure, and a 2024 European Court of Justice ruling that
goods harvested in Western Sahara entering the EU must be labeled with their own
territory of origin rather than as Moroccan.

**People and culture.** The population descends from Berber tribes, notably Gaetulian
and Lamtuna groups, mixed over roughly five centuries with the Maqil and Beni Ḥassān
Arab tribes; Islam arrived in the 8th century. The article covers Sahrawi refugee camps
around Tindouf, human-rights disputes on both sides of the Berm, and sections on
cross-cultural influence, gender relations, and artistic expression. Several sections
carry Wikipedia maintenance banners flagging outdated content, one noting information
current only to 2008.
