# Legislation Formalization

Legislation formalization represents statutory structures or rules in a
machine-checkable form so that tools can check references, calculate outcomes,
prove properties, or search for interactions. It applies
[[formal-methods|formal methods]] to models of legal texts; checking a model's
consequences does not establish that the model faithfully captures the law.

[[lexifina|Lexifina]] illustrates this distinction with US tax legislation:
cross-reference certificates, an executable model tested against examples and
other calculators, and searches built on that model. Version history and
effective dates matter because a consolidated text can contain amendments
that do not apply to the modeled year. The approach leaves human judgment
responsible for interpreting findings and assessing translation fidelity.
