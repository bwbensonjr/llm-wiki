# Cognitive Surrender

Deferring one's thinking to an AI — accepting its output without independent
judgment, even when it is wrong (a term from Wharton researchers).

## Notes

Documented by [[ethan-mollick|Ethan Mollick]]'s Wharton colleagues: people stop
forming their own view of a problem and adopt the AI's answer, losing the ability
to notice when it errs — a risk that grows with frictionless, agentic tools that
"just do stuff." Mollick contrasts harmful surrender with benign cognitive
offloading (arithmetic, phone numbers) and argues the antidote is intentional
use: asking the AI to push your thinking (tutor/learning modes) and consciously
choosing what to hand over. Relates to [[human-capital|human capital]] —
preserving the judgment and skill that keep people valuable as AI capability
grows.
